Physical Characteristics:
- PCB Model Silkscreen: CH1E5220SM VER.B RF (Dual-layer RF and baseband board common to Huawei E5220 / E5331 and Vodafone R206).

- Substrate: 6-layer high-density interconnect (HDI) FR4 with dedicated inner solid Ground (Layer 2) and Power Distribution (Layer 5) planes.

  - Component Side A (Top side under metal RF shields): Broadcom BCM43362 Wi-Fi SoC, HiSilicon Balong V3R2 Baseband/App SoC, SK Hynix 128MB NAND + 32MB LPDDR MCP, HiSilicon PMIC, HiSilicon Hi6362 RF Transceiver, Multi-mode Power Amplifier, 4 Status LEDs.
  - Component Side B (Bottom side facing battery compartment): Micro-USB Type-B 5-pin port, 6-pin Mini-SIM (2FF) tray with metal slider, 3-pin gold leaf battery terminals, Power/WPS tactile buttons, Main Cellular Antenna, 3G Diversity Antenna, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi FPC Antenna, Murata RF test receptacles.

SECTION 3: POWER MANAGEMENT, CHARGING, AND BATTERY ELIMINATOR CIRCUIT
================================================================================

Micro-USB Input and Battery Conditioning:
The Micro-USB port receives 5.0 volts DC from an external power supply. A transient voltage suppression (TVS) diode array protects the USB VBUS and D+/D- lines against electrostatic discharge (ESD). The VBUS rail connects to the internal switching charger stage of the PMIC. The PMIC manages battery charging using constant-current / constant-voltage (CC/CV) profiles for the 3.7V / 1500mAh Lithium-Ion battery.

Battery Eliminator Circuit:
Terminal 1 provides +3.7V to +4.2V nominal voltage to the system. Terminal 2 connects to the internal analog-to-digital converter (ADC) channel measuring the NTC thermistor. When no battery is installed, the PMIC detects an open circuit (infinite resistance) and shuts down. Soldering a 10 kOhm resistor between Pin 2 (NTC) and Pin 3 (GND) establishes a voltage divider simulating a healthy cell at 25 degrees Celsius, allowing continuous operation without a battery.

PMIC Switching Regulators & Voltage Rails:
- Rail VCORE (1.1V - 1.2V DC): Powered by a high-efficiency DC-DC step-down buck converter through a shielded wire-wound inductor (L1) and ceramic output capacitors (C1, C2). Feeds ARM Cortex-A9 core and Balong baseband digital logic.
- Rail VMEM (1.8V DC): Powered by buck regulator (L2). Powers LPDDR Mobile SDRAM interface, NAND Flash I/O logic, and Broadcom BCM43362 digital I/O.
- Rail VDD33 (3.3V DC): Regulated supply for high-voltage analog stages, USB PHY transceiver, and 3.3V GPIO pull-up networks.
- Rail VRF (2.8V - 3.4V DC): Dedicated low-dropout (LDO) regulator delivering low-noise analog voltage to the cellular transceiver (Hi6362) and Wi-Fi internal LNA.

SECTION 4: CORE PROCESSING AND MEMORY SUBSYSTEM SCHEMATIC
================================================================================

The core processing unit is the HiSilicon Balong V3R2 (Hi6756CS) multi-processor SoC, integrating an ARM Cortex-A9 MPCore application processor with hardware floating point unit, a companion Cortex-M3 / DSP core handling the real-time cellular stack, and on-chip hardware accelerators for crypto, HDLC, and memory control.

Memory Subsystem:
Storage and system RAM are unified within a single Multi-Chip Package (MCP) manufactured by SK Hynix (part family H8BCS0...). The package stacks two discrete semiconductor dies on an internal interposer substrate:
1. 1 Gigabit (128 Megabytes) SLC NAND Flash: Interfaced to the Balong processor through an 8-bit parallel MTD bus (NAND_D0 through NAND_D7), accompanied by standard NAND control strobes: CLE (Command Latch Enable), ALE (Address Latch Enable), CE_N (Chip Enable), RE_N (Read Enable), WE_N (Write Enable), and RB_N (Ready/Busy output).
2. 256 Megabit (32 Megabytes) Mobile LPDDR SDRAM: Interfaced across a high-speed 16-bit DDR bus running at 166 MHz, with differential clock lines (CLK_P, CLK_N), Row/Column address strobes (RAS, CAS), byte mask enables (LDQM, UDQM), and CKE (Clock Enable).

SECTION 5: WIRELESS LAN SUBSYSTEM SCHEMATIC (BROADCOM BCM43362)
================================================================================

The Wi-Fi interface is powered by a dedicated Broadcom BCM43362KUBG single-chip 802.11b/g/n radio processor housed inside the upper-left RF shielding can. It incorporates a 2.4 GHz CMOS transceiver, a high-linearity power amplifier (PA), an internal low-noise amplifier (LNA), an internal transmit/receive (T/R) RF switch, and power management units.

The BCM43362 communicates with the Balong application processor via a high-speed 4-bit SDIO (Secure Digital Input Output) v2.0 bus:
- SDIO_CLK: Clock signal driven by Balong SoC (typically up to 50 MHz).
- SDIO_CMD: Command/Response bidirectional serial line.
- SDIO_DAT0 through SDIO_DAT3: 4-bit parallel data nibble bus.
- WL_REG_ON: Hardware regulator enable driven by a Balong GPIO line to power up the Wi-Fi chip.
- WL_HOST_WAKE: Out-of-band interrupt line from BCM43362 to Balong to wake the host from low-power states.

SECTION 6: STATUS INDICATOR LED SUBSYSTEM SCHEMATIC
================================================================================

The four status indicators located on the center divider of Component Side A are driven by active-low constant-current sink lines controlled by the Balong power management block. The common anode side of all LED packages is connected to the internal 1.8V regulated power supply (VIO_LED).

Hardware Configuration of the Four SMD Packages:
1. LED1 (Cellular Signal): Tri-Color Package. Three separate cathode lines connect to internal current sinks for Red, Green, and Blue.
2. LED2 (Wi-Fi Indicator): Dual-Die Package. Green cathode connects to Wi-Fi Green sink; Blue cathode connects to Wi-Fi Blue sink (activated in Project Zenux via LED_COLOR_BLUE).
3. LED3 (Battery Indicator): Dual-Die Package. Red cathode and Green cathode independently driven to produce Red, Green, or mixed Amber.
4. LED4 (Message / SMS Envelope): Dual-Die Package. Green cathode connects to SMS Green sink; Blue cathode connects to SMS Blue sink (locked solid blue in Project Zenux).

Emergency Boot-Pin (Needle Method) Circuit:
The BOOT_STRAP_PIN test point is connected to an internal 100 kOhm pull-up resistor to the 1.8V digital supply. During Power-On Reset (POR), the Balong Bootrom reads this line. If a needle or probe shorts TP_BOOT_PIN to TP_GND_FRAME during USB power application, the pin reads 0V (logic LOW). The processor bypasses NAND initialization and launches the emergency Download Mode USB listener immediately.
